01 / UNDERSTAND THE DOMAIN

Start with what the professionals actually do.

I reviewed NFPA reference material, product gaps, and the workflows our customers needed: fire inspection, fire investigation, preincident planning, evacuation, and life safety.

The research became a product-content plan with specific libraries, symbol needs, and example diagrams rather than a generic list of safety icons.

02 / CREATE A WORKABLE SYSTEM

Structure content by task, not just by category.

I developed symbols and reorganized content so a professional could get to the right tools for their workflow, including preincident markers, safety devices, evacuation assets, and investigation notation.

Each library needed sensible naming, recognizable visuals, and a practical starting template.

03 / HELP THE STORY TRAVEL

A useful product still needs a clear picture.

I created original fire-preplanning imagery for SmartDraw’s promotional channels, including LinkedIn, Google advertising, and search-oriented thumbnails. These images were marketing assets, not customer templates.

Two different outputs: customer-facing product templates and separate marketing images. The promotional images are not templates in SmartDraw.

The drawings communicate the actual product use case with a floor plan, a route and relevant symbols. They were designed to make specialized content understandable at a glance.

04 / CHECK ADOPTION

Look at the specialized symbols, not only the category total.

In the four weeks following the March 26, 2026 expansion, recorded NFPA preincident symbol placements rose 38.6% and users rose 30.7% compared with the preceding four weeks.

That is an observed usage pattern, not a claim that the release alone caused those changes. It does show that the narrower professional content was being used.
