THE ORIGINAL PROJECT BRIEF

I was asked to look into life-safety plans. The deeper I researched, the clearer it became that fire inspectors, investigators, responders, and facility teams had different jobs to do—and our content needed to reflect that.

PROJECT DETAIL / 01

More use of specialized fire-planning content.

In the four weeks after the March 26, 2026 content expansion, NFPA preincident symbols saw higher placements and more unique users than in the preceding four weeks.

Monthly placements of NFPA preincident symbols peaked in July 2026 at roughly 2.6 times the March level and remained well above it in October.

The original fire-planning content shipped in October 2025; March 2026 was a later expansion.

PROJECT DETAIL / 02

“Fire planning” is not one user story.

The original ask was life-safety plans. I found gaps in pre-incident planning and other fire-related content as I investigated what working professionals needed. A facility manager preparing an evacuation plan does not need exactly the same diagram as a fire investigator documenting what happened afterward.

Rather than treating every possible fire-related role as a new feature, I narrowed the work to five important workflows and built a practical content plan around them.

Symbols and templates for documenting fire protection equipment, hazards, and building conditions.

PROJECT DETAIL / 03

I went straight to the source material.

I researched NFPA 1620, NFPA 170, NFPA 101, and other references, alongside competing products including First Due, FARO, FlowMSP, and Station Boss. The goal was to understand how a professional plan should be laid out and what information its symbols needed to convey.

I built the proposal around fire inspection, fire investigation, pre-incident planning, evacuation planning, and life safety. That gave me a way to decide what had to ship first without disappearing into every adjacent specialty.

PROJECT DETAIL / 04

Solve as much as possible without waiting for Engineering.

This was a product-content initiative: specialized symbols, templates, library organization, and appropriate shape data. I made most content with SmartDraw's own building tools and used Adobe Illustrator and SVG when the detail or customization requirements called for more control.

There were ideas that would have required additional Engineering effort, including an in-app satellite map selection workflow. That was outside what I could deliver directly, so I focused on giving customers useful planning content within the product's available capabilities.

I scoped the work, built the assets, checked them in QA and staging, and ran production checks after deployment. Another team handled deployment itself.

PROJECT DETAIL / 05

A launch I kept building on.

The initial work went live in October 2025. I continued adding content and improving the structure, with a further expansion and broader public marketing around March 2026.

SmartDraw now publicly describes fire-preplanning workflows, NFPA-aligned symbols, and fire-department use cases. Its public customer stories include fire services using the product for pre-incident planning and incident documentation. Those real-world use cases help show where the work fits into the broader product.

PROJECT DETAIL / 06

I made the visuals we used to introduce the work.

After building the fire-planning content, I created promotional artwork to communicate how the new content works: building layouts, fire-safety symbols, emergency equipment, and clearly marked routes. These visuals were made for marketing, including LinkedIn content, Google advertising, and promotional thumbnails—not as editable customer templates.

It let me contribute to both sides of the release—building the specialized content and helping Marketing show potential customers why it matters. I created the artwork; I did not manage the campaigns or claim their performance as my result.

PROJECT DETAIL / 07

Start with the people doing the job.

When I research a niche product category, I'm not trying to collect every possible feature. I'm trying to work out what each user must be able to accomplish, what fits the first useful release, and what can wait.

This project gave me a repeatable way to enter an unfamiliar professional field and build content that has a reason to exist.
