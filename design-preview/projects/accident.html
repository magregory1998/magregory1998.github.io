THE ORIGINAL PROJECT BRIEF

An enterprise customer's requirements shaped the first modernization of our reconstruction symbols. A second release focused on making those assets easier to use: accurate scaling, predictable growth behavior, better markup tools, and a clearer library structure.

PROJECT DETAIL / 01

A more usable system, organized around real workflows.

The July 2026 usability update reorganized more than 370 specialist symbols into 10 focused libraries, improving scale, resizing behavior, and markup. Subsequent usage reviews recorded activity across all 10 libraries.

The symbol and library counts describe content shipped in July 2026. The +97% placements and +117% unique users figures cover a narrower scope and should not be conflated with the 10-library review.

PROJECT DETAIL / 02

A detailed request, not a vague idea.

Sales was working with a prospective enterprise customer that had specific requirements for scene documentation. The team received a multi-page list of changes needed in SmartDraw, and I took the content requests through research, design, and implementation.

The emphasis was precision. Road geometry and vehicle sizes needed to be believable, annotations needed to communicate clearly, and damaged vehicles needed to show what happened at the scene—not just look like damaged cars.

PROJECT DETAIL / 03

A broken car needs movable pieces.

One request was for damaged vehicles. At first glance, that sounds like an illustration problem. In an accident diagram, though, the position of a bumper, mirror, wheel, or other debris can matter to how a scene is documented.

I drew vehicle variants in Illustrator, then drew their parts individually and aligned them so users could ungroup the symbols and reposition the pieces. Rear impacts, side impacts, and head-on damage required different versions, not one generic wrecked-car icon.

PROJECT DETAIL / 04

Get the scale and the markings right.

I worked on better-scaled vehicle types, roads and intersections, road markings, tire marks, crash-notation content, and layout details. The point was to give an investigator enough accurate, editable building blocks to document a scene clearly.

The September 2025 modernization expanded and refreshed the symbol content. The July 2026 follow-up addressed a different problem: usability. I corrected scaling and growth behavior, reorganized 370+ symbols into 10 purpose-built libraries, and added practical markup content so customers could work with the assets more predictably.

PROJECT DETAIL / 05

Two releases. Two distinct jobs to do.

First: modernization (September 2025). I redesigned and expanded specialist vehicles, road content, damaged-car components, debris, and annotation assets around real customer requirements.

Then: usability (July 2026). I focused on what happened when customers actually used the shapes—scale accuracy, resizing and growth behavior, clearer library organization, and more reconstruction-specific markup tools. The reorganized content was adopted across all ten libraries; the former library showed no recorded uses after the migration.

I owned the content research, build and QA within my remit. I did not own the broader product roadmap or the underlying analytics platform.

PROJECT DETAIL / 06

Crime-scene content has its own requirements.

I've also worked on crime-scene symbol libraries, including blood-pattern references, evidence illustrations, and articulated or repositionable scene content informed by forensic source material. That's related to reconstruction, but it's not the same project; I intend to give it a dedicated case study.

Both kinds of work reinforced the same lesson: professional diagrams have consequences beyond how they look on a screen, so the underlying detail matters.
