01 / CUSTOMER SIGNAL

A specialized request exposed general problems.

Sales brought a customer request requiring more accurate accident reconstruction work. I researched the use case and translated the requirements into detailed content, including road layouts, vehicles, collision notation, and scene elements.

The challenge was to make the assets both realistic enough for professional use and flexible enough for an everyday diagramming tool.

02 / FIRST RELEASE

Modernize the content, September 2025.

The first wave expanded and refreshed accident reconstruction symbols. I built roadway geometry, vehicles, damaged-car components, debris, and the visual conventions users needed to explain complex scenes.

That established a much stronger starting point, but it did not address every issue that appeared while people edited plans.

03 / SECOND RELEASE

Fix what happens after a symbol lands on the page.

The July 2026 usability work focused on consistent scale, predictable growth and resizing, added markups, and a cleaner content structure. I reorganized more than 370 symbols into 10 purpose-built libraries.

  • Scale accuracy
  • Predictable shape growth
  • Clearer library structure
  • More useful markup assets

The goal was to make specialist diagramming less frustrating and more trustworthy, not simply more decorative.

04 / UNDERSTAND USE

Look for sustained, broad use.

The post-release review recorded use across all ten reorganized libraries, including every symbol within them. There was no recorded use of the former library after the migration in the supplied analysis.

That breadth of adoption is encouraging, but it does not establish whether the release itself caused additional use. For a library reorganization, migration behavior and comparison windows matter.
