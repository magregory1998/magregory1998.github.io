01 / The starting point

The problem wasn’t just how the symbols looked.

Customers were reporting broken shapes. When I reviewed our architectural content against Rayon, AutoCAD, and CAD block libraries, a lot of it looked dated. Some drawings were hard to read, objects didn't always scale correctly, and customization behaved differently from one symbol to the next.

Meanwhile, the company's focus was largely on whiteboarding. I wanted to fix the floor-planning experience that many SmartDraw users already depended on. My Product Manager agreed that I could address the worst libraries while keeping my other work moving.

02 / The decision

Stop fixing duplicates one at a time.

What started as a handful of repairs became an opportunity to clean up the system. We had more than 40 libraries, with identical objects repeated under different room names. A search for “computer” could surface a dozen copies of the same thing.

I moved toward organizing residential furniture by what an item is—chairs, couches, tables, technology—instead of repeating it in every room. I kept specialized commercial areas such as healthcare, offices, restaurants, and kitchens easier to browse on their own.

03 / What mattered

A prettier shape that breaks is still a bad shape.

These assets had to work in the editor: resize to believable real-world dimensions, ungroup and regroup, support different color zones, and behave consistently when users customized them. I redrew modern appliances, furniture, healthcare equipment, commercial kitchen content, bar and restaurant symbols, security items, and architectural elements with those requirements in mind.

I also reworked examples where dimension lines made a drawing difficult to read. The test wasn't only “does it look better?” It was “can a customer actually work with it?”

750+ additions & redesignsThe count includes both newly created symbols and redesigned legacy symbols. It is not a count of 750 entirely net-new assets.
04 / Shipping it

Research, build, demonstrate, repeat.

I designed and implemented the libraries, tested the content through our release environments, and showed the work in progress to the CEO, CFO, and Product Manager. The reaction was enthusiastic, and leadership suggested more areas worth expanding.

The major content work was completed around mid-spring 2025, with further symbols, patches, drafting, and elevation releases later that year. Public SmartDraw announcements document the releases. A clear output is the scope of what shipped: redesigned, expanded, and reorganized floor-planning content. In the following year, our usage review also showed more distinct people creating floor-plan diagrams.

05 / The takeaway

Make it easy to use. Make it easy to maintain.

This project taught me that the structure behind an experience can matter just as much as the illustrations on screen. The work became a far stronger foundation for professional floor planning, at a time when SmartDraw was also investing more attention in that area.

My favorite part was turning dozens of disconnected problems into a more coherent system.
