01 / The starting point

A healthcare plan needs more than one bed.

When I reviewed what we offered for medical planning, we had little to build from. The absence wasn't just cosmetic. If someone wants to plan a clinical space, they need a useful set of recognizable elements and somewhere sensible to start—not a blank canvas and a single piece of furniture.

I took on the work of expanding that content: researching the kinds of medical symbols and templates our product was missing, then making them available in SmartDraw.

02 / My contribution

Research it, draw it, make it usable.

I was responsible for researching, designing, and building the new medical symbol libraries, along with healthcare templates. This is the kind of project where I don't just write a recommendation and hand it over. I work through the content problem myself, create the assets, organize them for use in the product, and check the end result.

The aim was a practical medical planning experience with more than one or two generic shapes. These are product building blocks, so clarity, scale, editability, and discovery matter alongside the visual design.

03 / Beyond the launch

Useful content can find an audience.

We didn't run a major marketing campaign around the new medical libraries. Seeing more people use medical symbols over the following months was encouraging—and a good reason to keep paying attention to what professionals needed from the experience.

A new place to start for healthcare planning.

Instead of a near-empty library, customers could work with a wider range of medical symbols and planning templates designed for actual clinical spaces.

04 / Using what I learned

Look at what people use, then keep improving.

I looked at symbol activity alongside healthcare template usage, rather than assuming the two would move together. It helped me think more clearly about the next opportunity: which medical assets are proving useful, how people find them, and what templates could help them get started faster.

That's the part of product content I enjoy—finishing the build, checking how the content is being used, and finding ways to make it more practical.

05 / What I took from it

Build the missing foundation. Then see what sticks.

What I like about this project is the full arc: find a big gap, learn the field well enough to address it, make usable content, and return to the data to see what people are doing. It's product work in a very hands-on form, even when the deliverables are symbol libraries and templates rather than a new engineering feature.
